# Crescendo

A fashion discovery companion for the girls who got tired of the scroll.

Working title. Internal document. Not for distribution.

## Manifesto

Crescendo is the slow exhale before the outfit. The pause where she remembers who she is. We are not a styling service. We are not a marketplace. We are not a feed. We are a mirror, a notebook, a small candle-lit room where she can hear herself think about what she wants to wear and, by extension, who she wants to be this week.

We believe taste is sacred. We believe taste is also learnable, but only in conditions of quiet. The internet has not been quiet in a long time.

We are building the room.

## Who She Is

She is twenty-two, or thirty-one, or somewhere honest in between. She has a Pinterest board called "in another life" and a second one called "what I actually wear." She owns one really good coat. She has unfollowed almost everyone. She reads Frankie at the cafe and saves the pages without dog-earing them, like a person who respects objects.

She is not a hauler. She is not a tryon. She has opinions about fabric and weight and how a sleeve falls. She is the friend the group asks before buying anything over one hundred dollars. She does not call this "personal style." She calls it "my thing," and she is still finding the edges of it.

She buys a candle sometimes instead of a top, because both of them are doing the same emotional work and the candle does not need dry cleaning. She knows the names of three small designers nobody she works with has heard of. She has at least one item in her closet that belonged to her grandmother and at least one item that belonged to a stranger.

She is tired. Not of fashion. Of the way fashion is sold to her.

She is not on TikTok much anymore. She is not, technically, against TikTok. She just noticed she didn't feel like herself after.

## The Feeling

The feeling we are designing against is the small ugly dopamine of the haul. The thumb-burn of the infinite. The vague shame of a cart full of things she will not love by Tuesday.

The feeling we are designing toward is the moment in the dressing room when something works and she doesn't have to ask anyone. The feeling of a season turning. The first cold morning when she reaches for the long coat without thinking. The feeling of being a person with a thing.

The product, if it works, will give her back ten minutes of her own taste.

It will also, on the better days, give her back a small kind of clarity about who she is when she is dressing for no one. Which is, in our experience, when the good outfits happen.

## World-Building

Crescendo is organized around energies, not categories. Not "tops." Not "bottoms." Not "dresses, casual, under fifty dollars."

The world is divided into moods that move with the calendar and the weather and her interior life. Autumn library girl. Salt-water summer. Urban witch. Soft western. Late-shift waitress in a Wim Wenders film. Sunday at her grandmother's. The energies are seasonal but not literal. They are lit from inside.

Each energy is curated, with a hand. There is an editor behind every one. There is no "for you" page that learned what she clicked on at two in the morning. There is a slow building of self.

Over weeks, the app learns which energies are hers, which ones she visits and never inhabits, which ones she returns to in October every year without fail. It does not surface this as a chart. It surfaces it as a feeling: she has a thing, and the thing has a name now.

## Texture and Tone

The interaction model has linen weight. Cards have warm shadows, not hard ones. Nothing pops. Nothing buzzes. The haptics, where they exist, feel like the click of a small wooden drawer closing.

There is no flicker. Loading states are warm, not anxious. The app does not show her how many notifications she has. The app does not show her how many followers anyone has. The app does not show her the time.

Type is set in a soft serif for headings and a quiet sans for everything else. The palette shifts gently with the energy she is inside. She does not pick a theme. The room dresses itself for her.

## Capabilities

We are resisting the urge to enumerate. These are not features. They are states the app makes available to her.

FR-1: She discovers her people through the rhythm of her saves, not their numbers. The app surfaces other women whose taste rhymes with hers, quietly, without ranking.

FR-2: She belongs to an energy. The app gives her a soft sense of where she lives, this season. She can leave at any time. She can have more than one home.

FR-3: She expresses her thing. Over time, the app reflects her taste back to her in language she did not have when she started. She gets her own adjectives.

FR-4: She is held by an editor's hand. Brand collaborations and emerging designer features arrive curated, with a byline and a point of view. Nothing is sponsored without saying so, and nothing is sponsored that we wouldn't keep without the money.

FR-5: She shops when she wants to, never because we asked. Outbound links to small brand sites are available, never required. We take no marketplace cut. We are not in the cart.

FR-6: She is alone with her saves. Her private boards are private. Her notes on a piece are hers. The app does not turn her interiority into someone else's content.

FR-7: She rests. The app has a closing time. It tells her to go to bed. It means it.

FR-8: She returns to herself. Once a season, the app offers her a small written reflection of who she has been in clothes, this quarter. Not a wrapped. Not a year in review. A page. A letter from the app, in the app's voice, that she can keep or delete without explanation.

## Visual Language

Warm whites. Bone. Dust. Tobacco. A red that has been left in the sun. The blues of a wool blanket someone's mother kept on the back of a chair.

Photography is editorial, not catalog. Hands in pockets. Half-faces. A girl from behind on a stone wall. We never shoot on a white seamless. We never use a model who looks like she has been paid to look at us.

Motion is slow. Transitions are dissolves, not slides. The app feels like turning the page of a magazine that someone made on purpose.

Sound, where it exists, is a single soft chime that has been described in user testing as "the noise a library has." We are keeping it.

Icons are drawn, not iconographic. Every glyph in the app was made by a human in one sitting. We will resist, forever, the rounded-square gradient app icon. Our icon is a small letter, set in a single weight, on a field the color of butter.

## Brand Adjacencies and Anti-Brands

Adjacent to: Frankie magazine. Kinfolk before it got austere. The Gentlewoman. Early Tumblr, the year before the porn ban. Pinterest in 2012, before the SEO. The Tom Bihn catalog. The way a really good independent bookstore lays out its front table. The Criterion Collection's color choices.

Anti-brand to: Shein. Temu. Revolve. The haul. The try-on. The infinite scroll. The five-minute styling reel. The discount code. The influencer's amazon storefront. Every app that has ever asked her to rate it on the app store.

We are not the next Depop. We are not the next Lyst. We are not, in any direction, the next Instagram for fashion.

If a press piece ever calls us "the Pinterest killer," we have failed. If a press piece ever calls us "where she goes on a Sunday," we have arrived.

## What We Will Not Be

We will not run ads.

We will not sell her data.

We will not show her likes counts on anything.

We will not have a comments section in the first year. Maybe ever.

We will not gamify saves.

We will not push notifications about trends.

We will not have a creator economy. We will pay editors directly.

We will not optimize for time-in-app. We will optimize for return after a week.

We will not be free forever, and we will not be ashamed about that.

## Signals We're Working

We will know it is working when she opens it on a Sunday morning with coffee. When she texts a screenshot to one friend and not to a group chat. When she stops buying things she returns. When she names her thing out loud at a dinner and the table nods.

We will know it is working when she misses it on a day she didn't open it.

We will know it is working when the press writes about it the way they wrote about Glossier in 2014, and we politely decline most of the interviews.

We will know it is working when a small designer in Lisbon sends us an email that begins, "I don't know if this is for me, but."

We will know it is working when a brand we'd never have approached approaches us. When she writes us a letter, on paper, on stationery she chose. When she shows up to a wedding in something she found in our pages and she does not credit us, because it has become hers and we are happy about that.

We will know it is working when the energy she names this October is one we did not invent. When the editors start chasing her, and not the other way around. When she has a thing, and the thing has a name, and the name is one she wrote down herself in the small private place we made for her notes.

It is for her. It is for them. It is, quietly, for us.
