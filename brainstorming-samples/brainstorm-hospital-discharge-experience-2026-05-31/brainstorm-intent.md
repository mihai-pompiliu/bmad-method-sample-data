# Brainstorm Intent — Hospital Discharge Experience Redesign

**Date:** 2026-05-31 · **Mode:** Autonomous solo brainstorm · **Ideas generated:** 62
**Techniques:** Five Whys, Metamorphosis Stages, Disney Method, Future Self Interview

## Topic
Redesign the hospital discharge experience so patients actually understand and follow their recovery plan — reducing confusion, medication errors, and avoidable readmissions. Scope is deliberately **non-software**: people, teaching methods, paper/physical artifacts, ritual, family involvement, the physical handoff, and the first hours at home.

## Goal
Find the few human/process/physical interventions that most move adherence and reduce readmissions — transforming discharge from a rushed paperwork dump into a designed handoff that sets the patient up to recover at home.

## Root Cause (the thing every intervention must address)
**The ward is rewarded for emptying the bed, not for the patient understanding.** The rushed final hour, verbal-only teaching to a foggy patient, paperwork written for liability, and discharge-as-ending all trace back to this single misaligned incentive.

## The Few Best Directions (priority order)

### 1. The Discharge Lounge (KEYSTONE)
A calm, non-clinical room (a repurposed side-room) patients move to the moment discharge is decided. Frees the bed immediately (leadership's metric) AND creates the unhurried hour for everything else. Fund it by framing it as a bed-flow/turnover tool so management defends it. This room is where directions 2-3 physically happen.

### 2. Fix the timing, not just the content (near-zero cost, high impact)
- Spread teaching across the whole stay: **one lesson a day** with the meal tray, beginning when recovery planning starts (not discharge day).
- Hard rules: **never discharge in the final 60 min of a shift; default to morning discharges**, hard-stop on evenings (daylight + open pharmacies/services to land into).

### 3. Replace telling with doing-and-proving (bundle three ideas into one protocol)
- Mandatory **teach-back**: patient (and caregiver) re-explain meds, warning signs, follow-up in their own words.
- **Physical practice** with a bedside **rehearsal cart** (real pill bottle, mock dressing, phone) — practice the 3 hardest tasks.
- Critic-proofed: this **replaces** the long discharge sign-off form (single "explained back: y/n" tick), so it saves nursing time rather than adding documentation.

### 4. Teach the caregiver, not just the patient (quiet breakthrough, near-zero cost)
Discharge cannot be scheduled until the **named home caregiver is present** (in person or speakerphone); the caregiver signs the teach-back. Optional: a "Caregiver Job Card" assigning meds/appointments/warning-signs roles like a pit crew.

### 5. Proactive outbound follow-up (humans, not a hotline)
A non-clinical **Recovery Coach** (trained volunteer / community health worker), named and introduced in person, makes a **scheduled outbound call** at the landing hour and again at 48h / day-7 — because the patients who most need help never call in. Critic-proof: rotating shift role ("today's Recovery Lead" badge), never one person who can be off sick.

### 6. Social-readiness checklist (the wildcard — may matter most)
A one-page **"Can you actually do this at home?"** check: food, working stove, transport, stairs, money, company. Catches the non-clinical failures that sink recoveries regardless of how well the plan was taught.

### 7. One physical artifact kept where life happens
Physically separate the institution's liability paperwork (filed envelope) from the patient's single usable object: a **laminated "This Is What You Actually Do" card / fridge placemat**, a **boarding-pass-style appointment card** (follow-up pre-booked with a named human), and a **day-by-day "recovery map"** showing what to expect each day including the normal day-4 dip.

## Key Supporting Ideas (lower priority, reinforce the above)
- Color-coded meds: sticker on the bottle = slot on the take-home tray = line on the plan.
- Pre-filled/sealed 7-day pill organizer + stamped bag to mail back discontinued bottles; "Brown Bag Amnesty" reconciliation at the bedside.
- "First Night Box" + "cocoon corner" setup guide for the first hours home.
- Shame-removal: nurse plays confused during teach-back; "Dumb Question Tokens"; peer "graduate patient" teachers.
- Ritual markers: 90-second doorway huddle, warm handoff walk to the car, "recovery roll" sign-out, day-3/7/14/30 milestone chart, week-3 hand-written postcard.
- "Worst-night rehearsal" + fridge traffic-light symptom card (green/yellow-call-coach/red-call-911).

## Notes for Downstream Planning
- Pilot candidate: a single ward, one surgical cohort. The Lounge + timing rules + teach-back-replaces-form bundle is the minimum viable redesign and is self-reinforcing.
- Every intervention here is non-software by design; a downstream digital layer (scheduling, reminders) could support but must not replace the human/physical core.
- Primary outcome metric to instrument: 7-day / 30-day stay-home (non-readmission) rate, replacing time-to-empty-bed on the ward dashboard.
