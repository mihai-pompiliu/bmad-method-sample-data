The Synthesis

What all 49 ideas were quietly pointing at.

Gatekept expertise

An expensive expert most people cannot hire.

Charged life moment

A transition with real emotional weight.

Reason to keep it private

Intimate, stigmatized, or yours alone.

The space a prompt pack structurally cannot occupy
The Moat

Expensive expert plus emotional charge

The strongest non-SWE modules all replace a gatekept human expert AND carry a life-transition charge. That combination, not the prompts, is the moat against generic prompt packs.

Distribution as a feature

Privacy-first is why it spreads

Many top ideas (divorce, sobriety, immigration, pro-se) spread underground precisely because they are private or stigmatized. Local, privacy-first AI is not a constraint here. It is the selling point.

Build as one suite

The End-of-Life journey nobody owns

Three modules are secretly one category. Connect them into a single journey that walks a family from caregiving through inheritance to final wishes.

Tending Elders→Generational Vault→Last Wishes
A reusable lens

The Shadow Profession as moat-detector

Run it over every future idea. If a module cannot name the expensive expert it replaces, it is a prompt pack, not a BMad module. Keep it as a permanent qualifying test.

Sleeper pick

Dungeon Master's Forge: the first public demo

The most visibly magical demonstration of multi-agent structure to a newcomer, and it spreads through evangelist hobbyist communities. Possibly the best first thing to show the world.

DEMO CANDIDATE
The unifying spec

The triangle, stated plainly

Gatekept expensive expertise + a charged life moment + a reason to keep it private. That is the catalog's north star, and the exact territory a generic prompt pack can never reach.

"BMad is not selling prompts. It is handing ordinary people the expert they could never afford, at the exact moment their life is on the line."
